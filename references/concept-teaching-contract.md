# Concept Teaching Contract

Use this when the book has important terms, models, distinctions, or frameworks.

## Concept Card

Each concept card must include:

- **Concept**: the term as used by the author.
- **Source anchor**: chapter, page, heading, or location.
- **Author's meaning**: the book-specific definition.
- **Plain explanation**: a beginner-friendly version.
- **Anti-definition**: what this concept is not.
- **Example**: one book example or source-grounded example.
- **Counterexample**: a case that looks similar but does not fit.
- **Nearby concepts**: what it depends on, contrasts with, or enables.
- **Common misunderstanding**: how readers usually flatten or misuse it.
- **Application boundary**: where it works and where it may fail.

## Teaching Order

Teach concepts by dependency, not by alphabetical order. If concept B depends on concept A, teach A first even if B appears earlier in the user's question.

## Precision Rule

Separate:

- what the author explicitly defines
- what you infer from usage
- outside knowledge added to help the user understand

Mark all three clearly when relevant.
